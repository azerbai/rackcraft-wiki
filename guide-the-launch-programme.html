The Launch Programme

A Launch Control touching a flat 3x3 Launch Pad (with open sky over all nine blocks) launches payloads. The rocket is sized to the job: one stage is a slim rocket about 14 blocks tall, two stages a Saturn IB about 19, and three stages a full Saturn V nearly five blocks across and about 60 tall, which needs a 5x5 of open sky over the pad. Players near the pad get the T-minus count on screen. Launch Tower blocks are see-through red lattice for building a service tower beside the pad; they're decoration only. Load Rocket Stages, a payload and Hydrogen Canisters (its tank holds 4,096; hoppers and Item Pipes can fill it), press Launch, and after a ten-second countdown the rocket lifts off. Every stage burns 128 canisters. About one launch in fifty fails: the stages and fuel are lost, the payload comes back, and two seconds after liftoff the wreck comes down on the pad and explodes. The blast is about 6, 10 or 16 blocks across for one, two or three stages, and up to half again bigger with a full tank left behind. It destroys the Launch Pad and anything plain nearby, sets fires and hurts anyone too close. Every other Rackcraft block survives, the Launch Control included. Build the spaceport away from home. launch.destructiveFailures (on) switches this back to fireworks only; launch.failureBlastScale (1) scales it.

Launches look like launches, drawn with the mod's own particles and visible from far off. During the count, vapour boils off the tanks. At T-3 the burn-off igniters throw sparks, then the first fire and a building cloud appear under the engines. At liftoff there's a flash, a wall of exhaust thrown out across the pad that billows up around the rocket, and long tongues of fire under every engine. The rocket leaves a white column of exhaust hanging in the sky for half a minute. Staging is a flash, a ring of smoke and a burst of fire. A failure is a fireball the size of the rocket, a cloud of soot and burning debris raining down. The ground shakes for anyone within about 320 blocks of a Saturn V.

Launches sound like launches too. Rackcraft's own synthesised sounds (no vanilla stand-ins) carry a mission-control countdown, a six-second ignition sequence from T-6 (igniters, turbopumps, building roar), a huge liftoff hit, the crackling roar of full thrust following the rocket up, the bang and ring of stage separation, and, for a failure, a proper explosion. Bigger rockets are louder and deeper: a Saturn V can be heard about 500 blocks away, and players further off (up to 768 blocks) hear a distant rumble. Camera shake follows the Distortion Effects slider.

Rocket Stages are built on the line too: a Stage Frame gets a weld, 4 Electric Motors, 2 Circuit Boards, another weld and rivets.

Strike Platforms and the Targeting Terminal

A Targeting Terminal fires the Strike Platforms you have put in orbit. It needs power, a fiber link to an Uplink or Core Router (the ground station), and the Orbital Defence project. Open it, type the target's X and Z, then type them again: the two must match. Check shows the biome and ground height, the distance from the terminal and from world spawn, the distance from every player, and whether the target would be refused. Pick Narrow (radius 24) or Wide (radius 48) and press Arm.

The terminal then charges the first ready platform. That takes 60 seconds at 5 MW, and longer if the grid can't keep up, so it is the strike or the rack hall. Everyone within 500 blocks of the target is told to leave, at arming, at 30 seconds and at 10 seconds, and sees the countdown on screen. Cancel works until the last five seconds. Then a needle of light comes down, swells into a white-hot column, and the ground is gone. Everything from the treetops down to a bowl 0.8 r deep is vaporised, and a shaft punches 2.5 r down through the middle, lined with magma, with lava at the bottom. The bowl's floor is glassed, glowing and burning. Out to 1.5 r anything that burns or shatters (trees, wooden buildings, glass) is blasted away and the ground is scorched. Everything alive in the bowl dies, Rackcraft machines included, and the rim burns. Only bedrock, unbreakable blocks and the block tag #rackcraft:strike_immune (empty by default, for operators to fill) survive. Each platform then cools down for 10 minutes; the terminal shows each one's timer.

Safety rails, because the game is often left running and people mistype: no targets within 128 blocks of world spawn, of any online player's bed or of the terminal itself; none in a chunk nobody has ever been in; the coordinates typed twice; the countdown cancellable. strike.enabled turns the whole thing off (a running countdown stands down), and strike.* holds every number.

Warheads

Built on the Assembly Line once Fissile Ordnance is done: a Sealed Waste Cask with 4 Enriched Uranium, a Guidance Chip (a silver- or gold-bin chiplet with a Circuit Board and Gallium Nitride, riveted), 4 Steel Blocks, a weld and rivets. A Military Base vault's Warhead Blueprint, handed to R&D, does half of Fissile Ordnance's compute. You can't use a warhead yourself. You need a Burner Phone (crafted: steel, a circuit board, a battery cell and a glass pane) and a fixer.

Use the phone with a warhead on you and the fixer picks up in chat. They ask where you are (your coordinates, filled in) and where you want it; a link fills in /burner drop <x> <z> for you to edit. They quote 1,000,000,000 RC and the warhead. Deal pays from the facility balance and takes the warhead and the phone. Call it off (before the drop) gets the warhead back and only a quarter of the money; the fixer keeps the rest for their time. Three minutes later, after chat warnings to everyone at one minute and ten seconds, a bomber crosses the target with contrails streaming and drops it. It falls nose-down for ten seconds, and then:

  • a flash, a shake and a mushroom cloud (a rising fireball that cools to smoke, a stem, a rolling cap and a base surge racing out along the ground), visible from far beyond the fog;
  • a crater 0.8 of the blast radius across (about 51 blocks) and over 20 deep, with everything above it gone. Out to the full radius of 64, everything weaker than stone above ground is flattened, stone too in the nearer half, and the ground is scorched, burning and patchily irradiated. People are killed inside the crater and hurt, burned and thrown further out. Rackcraft blocks and bedrock are spared;
  • the crater floor is radioactive rubble. Glowing Rubble in the middle (Radiation Sickness II if you stand on it, green light, green flecks) cools to Irradiated Rubble (Sickness I), which cools to gravel. It takes about six in-game days (warhead.rubbleDays), cooling on random ticks while the area is loaded;
  • fallout out to 96 blocks that eases off in steps over five in-game days (warhead.falloutDays). It gives Radiation Sickness III for the first fifth, II until half way, then I, shrinking towards the crater as it goes. Players inside see the level and the time left, and green flecks drift over the ground. It is saved, and it ends on time.

The light arrives before the sound: each client hears the blast and feels the shake only when the shockwave reaches it, at about 17 blocks a tick, so from a kilometre off you watch the fireball for three seconds in silence. Anyone within twelve blast radii who is looking at the fireball when it lights (within 60 degrees of the middle of their view, with nothing solid in the way) is whited out: the whole screen goes white for a few seconds and slowly clears, and their ears ring. Turned away, you only get a glow.

The fixer has standards: no drop within 256 blocks of any player, any bed or world spawn, and none anywhere nobody has been. Players can still walk into the zone afterwards; the warnings are there so they don't. warhead.enabled turns it off (a booked drop is recalled and refunded in full); warhead.* holds the price, refund share, delay, radii and fallout time. The bomber and the falling bomb are drawn by each client from one packet, so a drop works even where no one is near enough to load the area.

The fixer takes the warhead in your off hand, or else the biggest one you carry, and offers links to quote for the others; /burner drop <x> <z> <1-5> picks one. The bigger the warhead, the bigger the cloud, the longer it hangs, the further off it is seen and heard (sixteen blast radii, at least 1,024 blocks), and the longer the crater takes to carve: the blast front works outward over a few seconds for a Tactical, about half a minute for a Thermonuclear and a few minutes (with the server labouring) for the two biggest. A full Antimatter crater would be hundreds of millions of blocks, so the ground is reshaped out to warhead.maxCarveRadius (640) and dug warhead.maxCraterDepth (96) deep at most; past that the blast still kills out to its full radius and the fallout still covers its full spread. warhead.strategic*, warhead.thermonuclear*, warhead.cobalt* and warhead.antimatter* hold their radii and prices; the safe distance is the larger of warhead.safeRadius and the fallout radius plus 160.
