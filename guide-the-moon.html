The Moon

Grey, airless and a long way down from the station. Research Lunar Landing (after Crewed Spaceflight; 150,000,000 RC + 1.5B general) to get there. The highlands are pale Anorthosite under Regolith, broken by craters with raised rims; the maria are flat plains of dark Mare Regolith over basalt, the place to build. Gravity is a sixth of Earth's: you fall slowly and jump high.

Getting there and back. The Assembly Line rebuilds a Crew Capsule into a Lunar Lander (four Steel Blocks, sixteen Hydrogen Canisters, eight Electric Motors, four Cryo Coils, eight Circuit Boards, weld, rivet). Launch it (three stages) and it docks at a free Docking Port on your station. Go up, open the port and press Board Lander in a sealed suit with a minute of air: it sets you down in your company's landing zone with its Ascent Stage beside you. Use the Ascent Stage with eight Hydrogen Canisters on you to go back to the station, where the Lander docks again for the next trip. Cargo Pods can go straight to a Docking Port on the Moon: switch the Launch Control's destination (it costs one more stage).

The rules of orbit apply. Suits outside, Pressure Hull rooms with an Oxygen Vent to breathe, Radiator Panels to cool, and a Ground Link to reach the network. Sintered Regolith (smelt Regolith or Mare Regolith) is airtight, so you can build the base out of the Moon instead of aluminum.

Materials.

  • Ilmenite Ore, black and richest under the maria, gives Raw Ilmenite, which smelts into Titanium. Titanium makes the Titanium Helmet (30 minutes of air instead of 10), the Titanium Rocket Stage (counts as two stages for the same hydrogen), and goes into the Topological Core, Photonic Spine Router and Antimatter Warhead.
  • Lunar Ice turns up only near the poles: four make a block of Ice, which melts to water for an Electrolyser.
  • The Regolith Sifter, a processing cube that only works on the Moon, turns 32 Regolith into a Helium-3 Canister every 4 minutes per core at 300 kW. Helium-3 is fusion fuel.

The Helium-3 supply. A big Tokamak burns about a hundred canisters an hour, which is over 3,000 Regolith.

  • The Regolith Excavator digs it: set it on open regolith and it works the 9 x 9 patch round it for 30 Regolith a minute at 250 kW. It never runs out, but it isn't free. Each set of Excavator Teeth (two from four Titanium Ingots and a Steel Ingot) lasts 1,000 Regolith, so the Moon's ilmenite keeps you busy. The patch also tires as it's worked, sinking towards half the yield after a day or so of digging; breaking the excavator and setting it down on fresh ground brings it back. Mare Regolith yields a quarter more. Two or three excavators keep a big ring fuelled. Set it touching a Sifter cube and it feeds the cube directly; otherwise hoppers, Item Pipes or a Storage Link take the Regolith away. On an Item Pipe it fetches its own teeth.
  • Shipping it home. A Launch Control on the Moon sends Cargo Pods one way: home, to a free Docking Port in the Overworld, which unloads into the storage on its fiber like a station port. It flies only pods, on one stage, and needs a Heat Shield in a stage slot for the way down (eight Sintered Regolith around a Titanium Ingot; one burns per trip). The stage and the 128 Hydrogen Canisters have to be on the Moon already: ship stages up, and make the hydrogen from polar ice in an Electrolyser there. One pod carries 54 stacks. You can still carry it home, or reach Earth storage through a top Wireless Transmitter.

The Moon fights back, and every hazard has a counter.

  • The long night: a lunar day lasts eight Earth days, so the night is long and solar gives nothing. Radioisotope Generators (40 kW, day and night, no fuel) and batteries carry a base to sunrise.
  • Dust: every time an Airlock opens, dust gets into the room behind it, and racks in a dusty room mine up to 30% slower (Liquid-Immersion Racks are sealed). A powered Dust Filter clears a room in under two minutes.
  • Solar storms hurt anyone outside, suit or not. Get indoors; a Comms Satellite warns you a minute ahead. The flare at the front of a storm also goes for your hardware, in orbit as well as here (see below).
  • Micrometeorites punch through exposed walls. Whipple Shields stop them, or a second wall of Sintered Regolith.

Solar flares break things. Off the Earth, and only there, hardware can really break. A storm can come whenever you have racks up there, whether or not you're with them; everyone in your company gets the warning, and the Ops Terminal's alerts show the countdown and how much of your hardware is unshielded.

  • Shielding: a Whipple Shield or Sintered Regolith anywhere in the 24 blocks above a rack or a Ground Link protects it, so a shielded roof or a buried base covers everything under it. A pressurised room keeps people safe, but not hardware.
  • Racks: each unshielded rack with modules in it has a 60% chance of being fried. It shuts off, its front goes dark and it smoulders, and its modules are locked in until you choose: Space Repair Kits or salvage.
  • Space Repair Kits bring it back with every module intact. A repair takes one kit per tier of the best module inside, up to 4, all held in one hand. Each kit is a Repair Kit, 2 GPU Chips, 2 Cryo Coils and 2 Circuit Boards.
  • Salvage: sneak-right-click with an empty hand to strip the rack into Fried Modules, or break it and they drop. An E-Waste Recycler pays out by tier: I (Pi Node, 1U Server, ASIC Miner, Crafting Coprocessor) 4 Silicon and 2 Copper Wire; II (GPU Blade, Tensor Accelerator, Crafting Accelerator, FPGA) 8 Silicon and a CPU Chip; III (Quantum Core, Wafer-Scale Engine, NPU, Neuromorphic Core) 12 Silicon and 2 GPU Chips; IV (Photonic Tensor Core, Quantum Annealer, Topological Core) 16 Silicon and 3 GPU Chips. A stripped rack stays fried until repaired; break and replace it for a new one.
  • Ground Links: an unshielded dish hears only static for the 90 seconds of the storm, then comes back by itself. That's 1.25% of a two-hour Hosting Contract, enough to miss a 99% guarantee.
  • Satellites: a flare has a 35% chance of destroying one of a company's satellites, unless it has researched Radiation Hardening.
  • Tune it with events.flareFryChance and events.flareSatelliteChance in rackcraft.json; /rackcraft moon storm starts one.

Places. An abandoned Apollo-era landing site (descent stage, flag, rover, a plaque, and a chest worth opening) and the crashed lunar data center of Nebula Compute, a rival that got there first: suited guards, and loot that doesn't come back.

Land. The Moon is finite and shared, and its land is what companies fight over. A Claim Beacon claims the 5 x 5 chunks round it: inside them, no other company can break, place or use blocks. The first beacon to claim a chunk keeps it, and a company can hold four beacons' worth. Only you can break your beacon, to move it.

Operators can use /rackcraft moon go, moon home, moon board (board the nearest docked Lander), moon ascend (use the nearest Ascent Stage) and moon storm (start a solar storm now).
