Mining RackCoin and the Crypto Exchange

Racks mine RackCoin (RC) into one balance shared by the whole world. A rack mines when it has modules, at least 50% power, a fiber link to a router, and an intake below 40 C. Racks boot once powered: 2 seconds per Pi Node, 4 per 1U Server, 5 per ASIC, 8 per GPU Blade or Tensor Accelerator and 15 per Quantum Core, so a full rack of Quantum Cores takes two minutes. Mining, power draw and heat ramp up as they boot, and a rack that loses power starts from cold again. sim.rackBootScale in the config scales boot times. Open a rack to see its live RC/s, or the reason it has stopped and how to fix it. Spend RackCoin at a Crypto Exchange: its recipe is glass panes, two CPU chips, a circuit board, steel and an emerald. Its curated tabs sell bundles of resources, rare items and Rackcraft parts (ExchangeOffers.java). The All Items tab sells almost every survival item, searchable, priced from base values plus recipe costs (ExchangeCatalog.java); creative-only items are excluded. Click buys one, Shift-click ten, Ctrl-click a stack. Prices work like an exchange of value for value: raw materials have a base price, everything made from them costs what went in plus 10%, and then everything except plain building blocks costs double. Materials, ores, storage blocks, tools, parts and machines are what RackCoin is for, while stone bricks and stairs stay cheap. Rackcraft's own hardware costs more again, so a big balance can't simply buy a whole facility: components (chips, boards, modules, drives, motors, frames, tools) cost four times as much, and machines six times (exchange.componentPremium and exchange.machinePremium in the config). A Server Rack is about 1,700 RC and a GPU Blade about 23,000. Materials, fuels and vanilla items are unaffected.

Two counters. A new Exchange is the basic counter: materials, vanilla basics and Rackcraft hardware up to GPU Blades and ASICs, plus the machines a first facility is built from. Everything else it prices is high-tier stock: better modules (Quantum Cores, Tensor Accelerators), cryo coils, battery cells, nuclear materials, late machines (reactors, the Wafer Fab, Assembly Line robots, routers, launch hardware), netherite and rare vanilla goods (elytra, nether stars, totems, beacons, shulker boxes). It appears on the same Exchange once Prime Brokerage is researched (needs Extreme UV Lithography), at exchange.tierTwoPremium (5) times its usual price: a Chiller is about 110,000 and a Modular Reactor about 2.7 million.

Arms counter (optional compat). With Superb Warfare ReFabricated or TaCZ Refabricated installed, the Exchange also sells their gear, priced from their own recipes (compat/ArmsCompat.java, with no compile-time dependency on either mod). SBW guns are smithed from a blueprint, a material pack and a little extra. Blueprints are found, so they're valued by rarity, from 400 for a common one to 40,000 for a superb one. TaCZ's guns, ammo and attachments are one item each, told apart by NBT. Each Gun Smith Table result, from the default gunpack or any gunpack you add, is its own listing, sold with its NBT and surging on its own. Guns, launchers, attachments, perks, blueprints and material packs past common, heavy ordnance (missiles, bombs, rockets, cannon shells, mines, C4, drones) and any arms item worth five diamonds or more are high-tier stock. Guns, launchers and attachments also pay exchange.armsPremium (2). Ammo, grenades, parts and metals stay on the basic counter. Arms items no recipe prices (creative and loot-only) aren't sold. A TaCZ AK47 is about 15,000 RC, an SBW AK-47 about 130,000 and TaCZ 9mm about 4 RC a round. exchange.armsCounter: false turns the counter off. Modded ores and raw metals from any mod (lead, silver, tungsten, tin and so on, read from the c:/forge: tags) now get base prices too.

Rackcraft Quartermaster (companion mod, quartermaster/). It adds a Quartermaster Uplink that keeps the gun in your main hand stocked with ammo while you fight. Craft it from a Wireless Terminal, a Circuit Board, gunpowder and an iron ingot. Link it to a Wireless Transmitter (Shift-right-click the transmitter). Then wear it in its own Quartermaster Trinkets slot by Shift-right-clicking the air, or by placing it there in the inventory screen. A worn uplink stays on death. It also works from anywhere in your inventory, but there it drops on death like anything else. When the reserve, meaning ammo outside the gun, drops under one magazine, the uplink refills it to two magazines. It takes ammo from that storage first (hot drives only) and then, if set to, buys the rest on the Exchange out of the facility's balance. Right-click it, or press the Uplink Settings key (' by default) while it's worn, to open its settings: the mode (storage, then the Exchange, storage only or off) and the most it pays. It reaches as far as the transmitter does.

  • TaCZ: ammo goes into the gun's own reserve (TaCZ's "dummy ammo"), never your inventory. Extended mags count. When the uplink can't refill an emptied gun (it's off, gone or out of range), the gun switches back to loose ammo and ammo boxes.
  • SBW: ammo goes into the weapon's own reserve (SBW's "virtual ammo"), never your inventory. That covers pool ammo, rockets, grenades and missiles, in hand or at a vehicle seat.
  • Vehicles: restock in any game mode, since a vehicle's weapon loads from the vehicle even when you're in Creative. Hand guns in Creative never run dry, so they're skipped.
  • Datapack weapons: ammo still goes into the inventory, because vanilla bows only fire arrows from there.
  • Buying: stops at the uplink's price caps or when the balance runs out. Each uplink has a cap as a percentage of list price (starts at exchange.autoBuyMaxSurge, 150%, and goes up to uplinkMaxPercent, 1,000%) and an optional cap in RC a unit; the lower one applies. It can also leave a share of the balance alone: at 50% it stops once the balance falls to half of where it was when the uplink started buying. That reference starts again every uplinkBudgetMinutes (10), so the limit slows spending rather than freezing it, and each uplink keeps its own. The action bar says when a cap or limit stopped a purchase. Gun-mod ammo surges only exchange.ammoSurgePercent (0.1%) per round.

It also loads vehicle weapons. It covers the weapon at your seat in any Superb Warfare vehicle (and SBW add-ons), keeping vehicleReserveLoads (20) loads in the weapon's own reserve and refilling once it's down to half (vehicleRefillFraction), since cannons load one shell at a time. Vehicles without an ammo hold work too. TaCZ gunpacks and SBW add-ons work without setup, because the mod recognises any TaCZ gun or SBW gun and vehicle. Any other gun or vehicle mod can be described in a datapack at data/<namespace>/quartermaster_ammo/*.json, giving weapons (items or #tags), vehicles (entity types), ammo, optional ammo_nbt, magazine, rounds_per_item and unless_enchanted. A bundled file covers bows and crossbows (arrows, unless the bow has Infinity). With companies, an uplink only links to your own company's transmitter, and it spends your company's balance. It reaches both gun mods by reflection, so it builds without either. Settings are in config/rackcraft_quartermaster.json.

The vehicle dealership (Quartermaster, with Superb Warfare). The arms counter gets a Vehicles section: every vehicle Superb Warfare (or an SBW add-on) can assemble, on shelves by kind (ground, aircraft, boats, emplacements, civilian, utility). A vehicle costs its assembly parts at Exchange prices times dealershipMarkup (1.5), plus the ammo that comes in its hold at dealershipAmmoShare (25%) of its Exchange price, a bulk discount. What you buy is a Delivery Marker for it. Use the marker on open ground with room for the vehicle and red smoke goes up on the spot; a crate comes down on an orange chute (from dropHeight 90 blocks up, at dropSpeed 0.35 a tick, about 13 seconds), lands, opens itself and lets the vehicle out. It arrives fully charged, every weapon loaded, and with its whole hold filled with a mix of every ammo its weapons take, slot by slot (a tank's AP, HE and canister shells, its coaxial gun's rounds). A vehicle with no hold carries noHoldMagazines (20) magazines in its weapons instead. The marker refuses a roofed spot, a gap too tight for the vehicle, ground that isn't solid, and an aircraft in orbit. Typical prices: about 345k RC for an AH-6, 550k for a Bradley, 10M for a main battle tank, 13-15M for the big guns. Turn it off with dealership in config/rackcraft_quartermaster.json.

Surge pricing. Every unit bought raises that item's next price, compounding: 1% on the basic counter, 3% for high-tier stock (exchange.materialSurgePercent, exchange.surgePercent). The surge eases back by half every exchange.surgeHalfLifeTicks (4,000 ticks), so it is gone within an in-game day. A handful of anything costs about the list price; 200 Quantum Cores at once cost over sixty times as much as 200 at list price. Plain building blocks never surge. Everyone pays the same live price, including Site Planner drones and Auto-Buyers, and the Exchange screen updates as you buy. Automatic buyers never chase a surge: an Auto-Buyer, or a Site Planner buying without an approved quote, stops once an item passes exchange.autoBuyMaxSurge (1.5) times its list price and waits for it to ease, so a hungry factory takes a steady trickle instead of the whole balance. A Site Planner job you approved buys what it needs at whatever the market asks. Site Planner quotes are the whole lot's cost, surge included. Uranium is priced by the work behind it (2,000 RC for Raw Uranium, 50,000 for a Fuel Cell), and Spent Fuel, Waste Casks, Wafer-Scale Engines and the AGI's weights aren't for sale.

Rackcraft x Superb Warfare: Grid Charger (sbw-charger/, needs SBW). It adds a Vehicle Charger, a Rackcraft machine you put on a power network. Craft it from steel, copper wire, circuit boards, a PDU and an SBW Charging Station. It turns grid power into SBW vehicle energy at energyPerKj (250) per kJ, drawing up to chargerKw (200 kW) while anything needs charging and idling at 0.5 kW otherwise. A 5M-energy tank fills in about 100 s. A bigger battery raises the rating in proportion (scaleEnergy, 5M), up to maxChargerKw (2,000 kW), so an AC-130H's 40M tank draws 1,600 kW and also fills in about 100 s. It charges every SBW vehicle within radius (24 blocks) wirelessly. A vehicle with a Grid Link in its inventory also charges from its company's chargers up to linkRange (512 blocks) away, losing linkLoss (25%) on the way. Make the Grid Link from copper wire, circuit boards, steel and an SBW Battery Cell; it answers to the company of whoever last carried it. In Low Orbit or on the Moon, a solar storm cuts Grid Links until it passes, unless the charger has a Whipple Shield or Sintered Regolith overhead (flareCutsLinks). Power is shared evenly between every vehicle that needs it.

Vehicle service. A vehicle parked within serviceRadius (12 blocks), moving slower than serviceMaxSpeed and not hit in the last serviceQuietSeconds (10) gets repaired at repairEnergyPerHealth (5,000 energy per health, with up to half the charger's power if anything also needs charging), and its hold refills from Storage Arrays and Tape Libraries within 64 blocks at serviceAmmoPerStep (8) a step: stacks of its ammo are topped up, then empty slots take each ammo in turn. It only takes what's in storage and never buys. A company's charger only stocks vehicles carrying that company's Grid Link; a default-company charger stocks any. service turns it off. Settings are in config/rackcraft_sbw.json.

  • What's there: mods (items, blocks and entities per mod), find item|block|entity <text> (by id or name; click a line to copy the id), dump <namespace|*> (every item, block with its properties, entity and block entity type).
  • What it is: inspect (the block or entity you look at, with its NBT), nbt hand|block <pos>|entity <target>, and check entity <target> <path> <op> <value>, check block <pos> <path> <op> <value> and check count <targets> <op> <n>, which compare against an NBT path (= != < <= > >= exists) and return 1 when true.
  • Scenes: platform [radius] [block] (a flat floor, clear air, noon), clear [radius] (non-player entities), gallery blocks|entities <namespace> [text] (one of each in a grid; mobs stand still), give <namespace> [text], hold <item> [count].
  • Acting as the player: ride <entity> (through the vehicle's own interaction, forcing it if that refuses), dismount, use [pos] [face] [sneak] (a real right-click with the held item), useitem, attack <target>.
  • Workflows: script run <name> runs rcdev/scripts/<name>.txt a step a tick: commands, wait <ticks>, and assert <command>, which passes when the command returns more than 0. The log gets RCDEV_ASSERT PASS|FAIL lines and RCDEV_SCRIPT DONE ... failures=N.

Airports (same submod). New Overworld chunks can generate an airport: a dead-level runway 20 wide and 5,000 long, at sea level + 8. It has a stone embankment over water and valleys, 40 blocks of sky cut clear above it through hills, painted markings, edge and threshold lights, and striped blast pads. Taxiways lead to a lined apron with three Vehicle Chargers (they need power, and the first company to use one claims it, like a ruin's machines), two hangars, a glazed terminal and a control tower. Both the tower cab and a hangar have SBW blueprint loot. There's at most one per 6,144-block region, at a 50% chance, so a chunk only has to check its own region and the runway can be any length. /airport locate finds the nearest. Ground explored before airports existed won't have one, so /airport build (operators) builds one where you stand, along the way you face, level with your feet. It works a couple of chunks a tick, generating any that don't exist yet, and never touches a Rackcraft machine, a container or bedrock. Settings are in config/rackcraft_sbw.json (airports, airportRegionSize, airportChance, runwayLength, runwayWidth, runwayHeightAboveSea, airportClearance, airportBuildChunksPerTick).

Addon machines. Other mods can add Rackcraft machines with dev.rackcraft.api.RackcraftMachines.register(id, settings, behavior). The machine joins networks, belongs to a company, gets the status screen and steps with the simulation; its Behavior gives its kW demand and gets called with the power it received each step.
