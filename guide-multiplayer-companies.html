Multiplayer: Companies

On a server, players run companies. Anyone who hasn't founded or joined one runs the world's default company, which owns everything built before companies existed. Singleplayer worlds and old saves work exactly as before.

  • Commands: /company create <name>, invite <player>, join <name>, leave, kick <player> (leader), rename <name> (leader), color <colour> (leader), pay <amount> <name>, list and info [name].
  • What's separate: each company has its own RackCoin balance, research, AI business, orbit, warheads, orbital strikes, darknet auctions and HEROBRINE-1, and gets its own advancements and news.
  • One balance in every dimension: a company's balance, research, orbit, Exchange prices, AI business and darknet are the same in the Overworld, the Nether, the End and any other dimension, so racks anywhere pay into one balance. Older saves kept a separate, hidden balance and orbit per dimension; on load they're added to the main ones.
  • Ownership: a machine works for the company of whoever built it, whether a player placed it or a Site Planner's drones, a skid or a rocket made it. It mines into that company's balance and runs on its research.
  • Rivals are locked out: they can't open or configure your machines, see your drives (even on shared fiber), link a Wireless Terminal or Uplink to your transmitter, or ride your teleport pads.
  • Sabotage is allowed: the world is shared, so rivals can still break blocks, cut cables, smog you out, shoot and strike. On a server with PvP on, your armed Sentry Turrets fire on rival players in range (companies.sentriesTargetRivals).
  • What's shared: Exchange prices (buying anything raises its price for everyone) and the bounty board (first company to make the kill gets paid).
  • Teams: each company is a scoreboard team (rc_<id>). Members share a colour and name-tag prefix, are grouped in the tab list, and can't hurt each other.
  • Leaving: you keep nothing you built; it stays with the company. When a company's last member leaves, it closes and its machines go back to the default company.
  • Ruins are finders keepers: machines in Rackcraft structures (abandoned data centers, the Hyperscale Campus, the Military Base, the Moon sites) generate unclaimed. The first company to use one (open it, link to it, ride it) claims it, free, so finding a ruin early is a lucky head start. Before 2.6.0 they all went to the default company and nobody else could use them. In old saves those count as unclaimed too, until a default-company player uses one (companies.legacyRuinsClaimable, on by default). Jade shows a machine's owner, or "Unclaimed".
  • FTB Teams: with FTB Teams installed, FTB parties run membership. A player in a party is in the party's company, with the same name and members, and the party's owner leads it. The first time a party member joins with a company of their own, the party adopts it (balance, research and machines included); otherwise the party gets a new company. Leaving the party leaves the company. Players who aren't in a party keep whatever company they have, so turning this on never closes a company. While it's on, /company create, invite, join, leave, kick and rename point you to /ftbteams party. companies.followFtbTeams: false turns it off.
  • Operators can hand machines over with /company reassign <pos> [<to>] <company>: one block, or every machine in a box up to 512 blocks across. <company> is a company's name, default, or unclaimed.
